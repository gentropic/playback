Forme

The print side of the GCU design language. Where Switchboard dresses screens, Forme sets ink on paper — one trim, one ink, one signal, three components. Flip the switch above: every meaning survives in black and white.

Principle

Information lives in shape, weight, position and label. Colour is a removable garnish — signal never carries meaning alone, so [bw] drops it without losing a thing.

Paper, one ink, one signal. In black-and-white mode signal collapses onto ink, and ink darkens from slate to true black for clean toner.

Stamp — \fmlabel{}
An ink-filled box with reversed mono text, struck onto the page like a rubber stamp. Built from ink + paper, never signal.
Timeline — \fmtimeline
A quiet second track along every foot. \fmticks sets the marks; \pagebeat{} places the signal dot and caption. Read the foot straight through and a parallel story unfolds.
Fore-edge index
A tab whose height tracks the page fraction. Closed, the book shows a diagonal staircase down its fore-edge — a flip-aid, free from the same overlay.

Components — Hatches

Tone without colour

Four fills drawn in ink, so they read in pure black on a photocopier — texture that survives the [bw] master where a grey tint would wash out. \fmhatch{style}{w}{h}, or \draw[style] on any path.

In situ

A spread

Mirrored margins put the gutter on the bound edge; the folio rides the outer corner, raised clear of the timeline; the fore-edge tab sits opposite the gutter.

Kinship

The same voice, on paper

Forme and Switchboard share a palette, a type pairing, and a temperament — cool, instrument-panel, information-first. Switchboard has hover, focus, and motion.

Forme has gutters, folios, fore-edges, and a black-and-white press master. They are the same language spoken in two media: one lit from behind, one lit from the room.
